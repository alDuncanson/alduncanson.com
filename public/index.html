This is a website.

It is a small collection of documents written by Al Duncanson and connected by hyperlinks. It has no application to install, account to create, or notification bell to ring. You can simply read it.

Eventually, most of what lives here will be writing: short thoughts, technical explanations, advice held provisionally, and things learned by looking more closely. There will also be small experiments with browsers, servers, and the protocols joining them together.

Writing for people

These pages should be comfortable to read, quick to load, and useful without JavaScript or even CSS. The visual design uses warm browns and creams, but the structure is ordinary semantic HTML.

Simple does not have to mean sterile. A document can have character without demanding more of its reader.

Links remain underlined, emphasis remains emphasis, and important words remain unambiguous. Even a small piece of inline code such as printf 'hello, web\n' should sit comfortably inside a sentence.

Learning from the machinery

The site itself is part of the subject. Curious readers are invited to inspect its source, request its documents in different forms, and see what the server says when asked a direct question. 

curl --include https://alduncanson.com/
curl --compressed https://alduncanson.com/humans.txt
curl https://alduncanson.com/coffee

Some principles, while this place takes shape:

  • Documents should have durable, meaningful addresses.
  • The source should reward inspection.
  • HTTP features should be used deliberately and explained openly.
  • A page should justify every byte it asks someone to download.
