Interpunct

While writing the hypertext for the footer on my homepage, I wondered not just what character can I use for separation, but what character should I be using?

A pipe? Perhaps, I've done it before:

sitemap | source | humans.txt | ☕︎ | code

Sure, that could work—but, it doesn't feel right.

Maybe a black circle:

sitemap ● source ● humans.txt ● ☕︎ ● code

Oof, nope. Way too big.

Oh I know, a bullet:

sitemap • source • humans.txt • ☕︎ • code

Hmm, that's closer to what I wanted but, that didn't feel right either; bullets are for delimiting items in unordered lists.

They don't look right when used this way. Still too big and too bold. I wanted something a little quieter, something a bit cleaner, a bullet—refined.

A dot! That's what I want:

sitemap . source . humans.txt . ☕︎ . code

Well, we're getting somewhere.

A period gives me a dot, but it didn't look right either; I wanted the dot that the period gives me visually, but up a little bit, in the middle… a middle-dot.

I consulted the all-powerful search engine: "how to type middle dot", and was guided, rather reluctantly by the dreaded "AI Overview" (the summarization of the results typically returned by a search engine, this is 2026) to type ⌥ + Shift + 9 (I'm on MacOS, for those confused by ⌥).

I decided to trust the instructions, regardless of what could be said of their credibility and accuracy right now (again, it's 2026 iykyk).

Seemed simple enough, ⌥ + shift + 9:

9

Hmm, that didn't work. Maybe I typed it incorrectly.

Staring down at my fingers as I prepare them to press each key sequentially, hands of a surgeon, focused, I type again ⌥ + shift + 9:

9

Blast!

Alright, maybe it's my editor that's the problem…Neovim, btw.

I consulted the oracle again in my caveman-esque pre-AI algorithmic dialect—keywordese: "how to type middle dot neovim".

Ha, of course. The wonderful community of builders, users, and maintainers of Neovim already have a solution for my problem, digraphs:

Digraphs are used to enter characters that normally cannot be entered by an ordinary keyboard. These are mostly printable non-ASCII characters.

Amazing.

Until now I had been searching, copying, and pasting the unicode symbols from my browser into my editor.

I searched the page for the elusive middle dot I sought after, ⌘+f "middle dot"…

Direct hit.

·	.M	0xb7	183	MIDDLE DOT

Aptly named MIDDLE DOT—lucky for me—and the digraph is .M.

To use a digraph in Neovim, the incantation is:

CTRL-K {char1} {char2}

Once again, with the hands of Dr. Strange before his accident, I type ⌃+k . shift+m, and voila:

·

Oh my god, it's perfect.

I yanked and put (putted? pasted?) this magical little symbol between each element, and:

sitemap · source · humans.txt · ☕︎ · code

It's… beautiful.

I may be biased, since I had spent my entire evening researching this little symbol, but I think that this is the best character symbol for delimiting horizontal lists of elements. And it seems I'm not the only one that thinks so!
